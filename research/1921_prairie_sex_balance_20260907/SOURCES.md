# Sources and research decisions

Consulted on 7 September 2026. This is a targeted literature examination, not a systematic review. The draft does not claim that absence from these searches demonstrates novelty.

**Principal evidence.** Dominion Bureau of Statistics, *Sixth Census of Canada, 1921*, volume I (Ottawa, 1924), Table 16, pp. 249–339. [Original volume](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS98-1921-1-1924.pdf). Downloaded and inspected using its text layer and page images. All nine values in the six city rows recorded in `verify.py` were checked visually. The analyzed edition is the [HGIS Canada source workbook preservation](https://jimclifford.ca/hgiscanada/sources/1921_V1T16_PUB_202306/) of the Canadian Peoples/TCP data. [HGIS attribution and scope](https://jimclifford.ca/hgiscanada/about/). The main statistical results are new calculations from the user's supplied local data.

**Digitization documentation.** Canadian Century Research Infrastructure, [Digitized Published Tables](https://ccri.library.ualberta.ca/endatabase/geography/digitizedpublictables/index.html). Read the methodology and table inventory. The documentation identifies Table 16, describes validation and editorial notes, and defines city and village type codes. This informed the source critique and classification audit; it is not evidence that every source value is historically correct.

**Closest contemporary predecessor.** Dominion Bureau of Statistics, *Origin, Birthplace, Nationality and Language of the Canadian People: A Census Study Based on the Census of 1921 and Supplementary Data* (Ottawa, 1929), prepared under W. Burton Hurd's direction. [PDF](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS98-1921M-4-eng.pdf), [catalogue](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.832189/publication.html). Downloaded; examined contents, preface, pp. 71–72 and 111–12. Its discussion of urban concentration within immigrant groups sharply limits a claim of conceptual novelty. Full volume not read.

**Later demographic interpretation.** Joseph A. Norland, *The Age-Sex Structure of Canada's Population*, 1971 Census profile study, catalogue 99-703 (Ottawa, 1976), pp. 11–12 and Table 3. [PDF](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS99-703-1971.pdf). Read the relevant pages online. Birthplace within and outside the province and immigration-period breakdowns warn against attributing the entire Canadian-born component to an unspecified migration mechanism. Full study not read.

**Gender historiography.** Lindsey McMaster, *Working Girls in the West: Representations of Wage-Earning Women* (Vancouver: UBC Press, 2007), introduction, particularly pp. 3–8. [Publisher's sample](https://www.ubcpress.ca/asset/9141/1/9780774814553.pdf), [book metadata](https://www.ubcpress.ca/working-girls-in-the-west). Read the relevant introduction in the sample. The argument treats representations and the domestic ideal; the present census table does not identify the population of workers studied. Full book not read.

**Method.** Evelyn M. Kitagawa, “Components of a Difference Between Two Rates,” *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 50, no. 272 (1955): 1168–1194. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1955.10501299). Verified bibliographic record. The standard symmetric accounting identity is provided and independently checked in this project's code; no claim is made to have read the paywalled full article.

**Additional context consulted, not used to establish priority.** [The Encyclopedia of Saskatchewan, “Women of Saskatchewan”](https://esask.uregina.ca/entry/women_of_saskatchewan.html), especially the paragraphs on urban employment. [1931 census monograph on rural and urban population](https://www.publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS98-1931CM-6-eng.pdf), selected passages on sex distribution and women's employment. [Historical Statistics of Canada, Section A](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-516-x/sectiona/4147436-eng.htm), table inventory. [Sarah Carter, *Imperial Plots*, chapter seven](https://escholarship.umanitoba.ca/read/imperial-plots-women-land-and-the-spadework-of-british-colonialism-on-the-canadian-prairies/section/c2782832-0ab4-46aa-b19f-b15d080e5cf0) was located but the full text was not successfully retrieved; no substantive claim relies on it.

Search themes included Canadian census sex ratios and birthplace in 1921; Canadian-born urban and rural populations; Prairie women, employment and migration; historical demographic analyses; and the original Table 16. Searches found broad and close predecessors but no verified study with this exact city selection and decomposition. That is a record of the search result, not a priority claim.

The analytical sequence was exploratory: read the handoff; inventory current census variables; select the 1921 sex-by-birthplace table; examine provincial and major local counts; identify the city/remainder question; calculate the decomposition; search for close predecessors; test alternative definitions and local comparisons; verify evidence; write the draft. The city distinction was chosen after observing the local counts. It was not specified independently of the observed data. British Columbia was inspected during scoping but excluded from the final Prairie question; fragmented city reporting there would require a different partition. No alternate historical question was analyzed to a completed result and then hidden.

Original repository research papers, older benchmarks and the pre-existing `paper_analysis/` directory were not read. External published history and census analysis were consulted specifically to avoid claiming established findings as discoveries. There was no external reviewer or subagent during this draft; the review request is for the user's proposed Fable assessment.
