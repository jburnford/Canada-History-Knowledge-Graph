# Independent review request for Fable 5.1

Please review `DRAFT.md` (or `DRAFT.pdf`), with `METHODS.md` and the attached result tables. This is an independently developed exploratory census study. The researcher did not consult old papers or analyses in the user's repository. Your task is to assess the historical contribution, not reward autonomous production or numerical sophistication.

The principal result is that the three broad birthplace shares account for −0.210 percentage points of a 4.783-point city–remainder difference in male population share on the Canadian Prairies in 1921. Differences within those groups account for 4.993 points. The Canadian-born contribution is 2.466 points. The selected seventeen cities contain 23.5% of the population and 2.6% of its net male surplus. Fourteen cities have Canadian-born female majorities. Sensitivities and fourteen local division comparisons support the broad pattern.

The strongest novelty objection is already visible: the Dominion Bureau of Statistics' 1929 birthplace monograph, pp. 111–12, explicitly recognized greater urban concentration of females within immigrant birthplace groups. Norland's 1976 age-sex study also discussed the contribution of internal and international migration to regional sex ratios. The draft claims a quantitative extension and local testing, not discovery of those mechanisms. It cannot establish priority through its limited literature search.

Please address these questions directly:

2. Does the argument distinguish the census's “foreign-born” category from everyone born outside Canada, city status from urban population, and an all-age imbalance from a marriage-market or labour-force measure?
3. Could age composition or heterogeneity within the three birthplace groups substantially change the historical interpretation? Does the draft restrict its claims adequately?
4. Do the population partitions, province subtraction, blank-cell treatment, within-division comparisons and sensitivity choices support the headline finding? Identify any accounting or source error.
5. Is the Canadian-born emphasis illuminating or misleading given children, locally born residents, interprovincial migrants and Indigenous people in that category?
6. Recommend reject, revise substantially, revise modestly, or accept as a research note. State the three changes that would most improve the paper and which claims should be removed.
