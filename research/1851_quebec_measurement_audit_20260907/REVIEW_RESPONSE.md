# Adversarial review — verdict: REDESIGN

Reviewer: Claude (Fable 5), 7 September 2026. Independent checks run against the repository, the workbook at the hashed path, the printed page image, and the article's open supplementary PDF. `audit.py` was rerun from the repo root and reproduced all four output files byte-for-byte.

## Strongest objection first

**As designed, the aggregate census inventory cannot separate "restricted farm establishment" from "attractive outside opportunities," and the predecessors who could separate them already did so with the linked micro-data this project lacks.** Gérard Bouchard's Saguenay project (BALSAC-linked family reconstitution; *Quelques arpents d'Amérique*, Boréal, 1996, and his earlier *Histoire sociale/Social History* articles on family land transmission) is precisely a study of who could establish a farm and where the next generation went, conditional on local opportunity — with observed inheritance, establishment, and out-migration at the family level. Dessureault (cited in the memo) does the wealth/occupation side for Saint-Hyacinthe. At county aggregates, both remaining mechanisms predict the same observable — young-adult cohort deficits correlated with land-availability proxies — because constrained places and attractive destinations are connected by the same transport corridors and the same regional labour markets. The memo's design table implicitly concedes this ("farm acquisition or inheritance evidence corroborates," "household and notarial evidence would be particularly useful") but the proposed inventory contains none of that evidence. Without it, the project reproduces the Bouchard/Dessureault question minus their identification, and fails its own novelty standard. That is a redesign problem, not a rejection: the fix is to scope the aggregate work as case *selection*, and commit to a micro-evidence component (notarial acts, parish/BALSAC linkage, or US-side destination data) for a small number of matched county pairs before any mechanism claim is made.

## Answers to the five questions

**1. Is the three-mechanism distinction a discriminating test?** The taxonomy is not new — it is the organizing frame of the whole crisis debate (Ouellet vs Paquet–Wallot on production; McCallum's *Unequal Beginnings* on land and markets; the push/pull framing is a century old in the Franco-American emigration literature). What *could* be discriminating is the conditional design — departures conditional on production opportunity — but only if land access is measured independently of production. At county aggregates it largely cannot be: farm-size distributions, cultivated shares, and occupier counts are joint outcomes of the same settlement process. Verdict on Q1: a familiar thesis; the discriminating power must come from data the current inventory does not contain.

**2. Closest predecessors (verify citations before use; given from reviewer knowledge, not fetched):** Bouchard, *Quelques arpents d'Amérique* (1996) and his family-transmission articles — the direct anticipation of the establishment mechanism; Ramirez, *On the Move* (1991) — outside opportunities; Lavoie, *L'émigration des Québécois aux États-Unis* — cohort-deficit measurement and its pitfalls; Lewis & McInnis, "The Efficiency of the French-Canadian Farmer" (JEH 40:3, 1980) — production; McCallum (1980); Greer, *Peasant, Lord, and Merchant* (1985) for pre-1840 establishment dynamics. Any novelty claim must be stated relative to Bouchard specifically.

**3. Is the treatment of Bond, Geloso and Swanson fair?** Yes — scrupulous, and partially now verified. The memo distinguishes the written equation from estimable date effects, declines to claim their software estimated an invalid coefficient, and correctly reports A3 (confirmed against the actual supplement). Remaining exposure: the main-text prose characterization and Table 4 are unverified; the collinearity point is contingent on the equation actually containing all three terms. Obtain the article before repeating either point.

**4. Can aggregate variables identify any mechanism?** Production failure, partially: persistent output deterioration preceding cohort loss is testable at aggregate level *if* crop substitution and the 1850s wheat-midge/potato shocks are handled, and if the 1851 (enumerated Jan 1852) and 1861 reference dates are established precisely. Restricted establishment and outside opportunities: not separable without (a) notarial land-transfer/inheritance evidence or family linkage (BALSAC-style) for matched cases, and (b) destination-side evidence (US census Canadian-born counts, mill-town employment, transport access). Everything correlating cohort deficits with farm-size structure remains ecological and must be labelled as such.

**5. Justifying result vs kill criterion.** Justify: on validated geography, matched county/CSD pairs with statistically comparable production trajectories but divergent land access showing divergent young-adult retention, *corroborated* by independent acquisition/inheritance evidence in at least a subsample — that separates establishment from production and survives the ecological objection. Kill: if unit-harmonization error (measurable with this dossier's own reconciliation machinery) is of the same order as the cohort-deficit signal; or if land-access and destination-access measures are too collinear across counties for any aggregate contrast to exist. In either case, publish the source-audit as a data note and stop.
